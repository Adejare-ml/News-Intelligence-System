Nigeria Sets New Stablecoin Licensing Rules and Capital Requirements

newsBTC · Sat 7 Nov, 23:00 Lagos

What happened

The Central Bank of Nigeria issued a new framework on November 5 for non-security virtual assets, creating separate licensing classes for naira-referenced and foreign-currency stablecoins. The rules set reserve, capital and local-presence obligations and restrict certain algorithmic designs. The framework also applies to regulated activities directed at Nigerian residents, even where the provider is based abroad.

Key facts

  • The Central Bank of Nigeria issued the framework in a November 5 circular and accompanying regulations.
  • The framework applies to regulated activities conducted in or from Nigeria or directed at Nigerian residents.

AURA analysis · interpretation, not reported fact

Why it matters: Stablecoin issuers and payment firms serving Nigerian customers face new capital, reserve and licensing requirements, including for foreign-based platforms. The framework also gives the central bank power to prescribe higher capital amounts.

Named: Central Bank of Nigeria

5 · Business · Earnings

Top NGX-listed tech companies ranked by revenue growth in 2026

Nairametrics · Sun 8 Nov, 16:05 Lagos

What happened

Revenue growth across nine NGX-listed technology and telecommunications companies was mixed in 2026, according to an analysis of their latest financial statements as of November 8, 2026. Omatek Ventures recorded the highest growth at 650% from a very low base, while Legend Internet posted the weakest performance with a 7.4% revenue decline. Several companies reported rising revenue alongside weaker profitability.

Key facts

  • Omatek Ventures Plc revenue rose 650% to approximately N1.5 million for the nine months ended September 30, 2026.
  • Legend Internet revenue fell 7.4% to N1.10 billion for the 12 months ended July 31, 2026.
  • MTN Nigeria revenue grew 23.6% to N4.61 trillion for the nine months ended September 30, 2026.
  • eTranzact profit after tax fell 42.7% to N1.38 billion from N2.41 billion.

AURA analysis · interpretation, not reported fact

Why it matters: The analysis gives investors and regulators a comparative view of revenue and earnings performance across listed technology and telecoms firms, showing that revenue growth did not consistently translate into profit growth.

Named: Omatek Ventures Plc, NCR Nigeria Plc, CWG Plc, Airtel Africa Plc, Legend Internet
